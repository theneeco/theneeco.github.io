How I think about it

Five beliefs that shape how I assess risk and advise the people who own it.

EDIT: this is starter text. Rewrite each one in your own words, keep the ones you believe and cut the rest. Delete this line when done.

Start from how things actually fail

Penetration testing taught me that attackers don't read policies. I test a control by asking how I'd get around it, not by checking that a document exists.

Evidence beats documents

A control that can't show it worked last quarter isn't a control yet. I'd rather see five controls with evidence than fifty with good intentions.

Write for the person deciding

A board needs the three risks that matter, what each would cost and the decision it's being asked to make. The technical detail belongs in the appendix.

Privacy is a design constraint

The cheapest privacy fix happens before the data is collected. I work with product and engineering teams early, so privacy shapes the design instead of blocking the launch.

Govern AI by use case

"AI" is too broad to govern. A chatbot answering member questions and a model deciding insurance claims need very different controls, so I start with an inventory of what's actually in use.
